## Description
One penetration made of three separate parts people confuse for each other. Bottom to top: (1) flashing cone / base flashing / flashing boot / roof jack — laces into the shingles, metal cone around the pipe; (2) storm collar — the ring clamped to the pipe above the cone, acting as an umbrella over the joint between cone and pipe, sealed with high-temp sealant; (3) rain cap / flue cap — see vent-cap.

## Replacement notes
Match wall type first, then diameter, then pitch. Cap should match the pipe manufacturer where possible.

## Measurement notes
Pipe OD, and whether it's single-wall or B-vent (double-wall) — these are sized differently and the parts are not interchangeable between them. Roof pitch — flashings come in standard and steep-roof versions. Brand — B-vent is a listed system; manufacturers say don't mix brands on the pipe and cap.

## Failure modes
Leaks at the cone neck and at the storm collar; wind flex cracks the sealant. Water runs down between the inner and outer wall, so it can show up far from the penetration. This is a common re-roof line item — cap, collar, and flashing usually get replaced together with the roof.

## Disambiguation
"Heat stack" is sometimes used for this whole penetration and sometimes for just the storm collar around the pipe. Usage is inconsistent and regional — not resolved to an alias here pending review.
