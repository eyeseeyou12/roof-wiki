## Description
Continuous vent running along the roof peak over a cut slot in the deck, letting air out the full length of the ridge. Three distinct types: (1) shingle-over — rigid, nailed down, then covered with hip-and-ridge cap shingles so the fasteners are hidden, the current default; (2) aluminum / metal exposed — the older style, common in the 80s and 90s, fasteners go through the surface and into the deck so they're exposed and rely on sealant that needs re-application, comes in 8- or 10-foot sections; (3) roll vent — flexible corrugated strip, rolled out and shingled over, easy on long ridges but generally lower net free area than rigid. Aluminum was common through the 80s and 90s and is what you'll find on older roofs; shingle-over has largely replaced it on re-roofs (20-year note).

## Function
Exhaust for the attic, distributed along the ridge instead of at points. Requires soffit intake to do anything.

## Replacement notes
Match type to covering first. On a shingle roof, a shingle-over vent is the like-for-like or the upgrade from aluminum. Metal roofs need a matched metal system.

## Measurement notes
Ridge length available (short or cut-up ridgelines are why some roofs still get static vents instead). Whether it has an external baffle — the baffle is what keeps wind-driven rain from pushing back over the ridge; worth checking on any ridge vent you're matching. Roof covering — metal roofs take a vented ridge closure or a vented ridge cap matched to the panel profile, not a shingle-over product.

## Failure modes
Aluminum vents get stepped on, blow off in high wind, and leak at the exposed fasteners once the sealant lets go. Shingle-over units fail mostly from bad install — slot cut wrong, or not run far enough.
