## Description
Electric fan in a domed housing on a flanged base, controlled by a thermostat and often a humidistat.

## Function
Mechanically exhausts hot attic air rather than relying on convection.

## Replacement notes
Same throat size. Two useful substitution paths: these are commonly sold as a replacement for a turbine or a roof pot, so a turbine hole often takes a power vent directly; going the other way, a dead power vent is frequently replaced with a static vent or a solar unit rather than another electric one. Thermostat/humidistat controls are replaceable separately — a fan that won't come on is often a control, not a motor.

## Measurement notes
Throat/hole size and base dimensions — standard bases are around 24" x 24", but measure. Control type: thermostat only, or thermostat plus humidistat. Roof pitch. Whether it's hardwired or plugged into a nearby receptacle.

## Failure modes
Motors die, and they die early — they're air-cooled and moving 120°F+ air. Noise. Thermal overload trips. The bigger critique in the trade is that a PAV without adequate soffit intake pulls conditioned air out of the living space, which costs more than the attic cooling saves. The performance criticism is contested — real disagreement in the trade, not settled fact — present as a consideration, not a verdict.

## Disambiguation
"Attic fan" gets used for at least three different things: a roof-mounted powered attic ventilator (this entry), a gable-mounted fan, and a whole-house fan (a completely different appliance that pulls air from the living space, not yet covered in this wiki).
