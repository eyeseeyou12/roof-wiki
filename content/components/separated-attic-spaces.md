## Description
A house can have more than one attic space that doesn't share air with the others — a garage attic cut off from the main attic by a firewall, an addition whose roof ties into the original roofline without the attics actually connecting, a bump-out or dormer with its own small enclosed space. Each one needs its own ventilation calculation, sized and balanced independently. Ventilating one well doesn't help another that has no path to it.

## Function
The test isn't whether the roof looks like one continuous surface from outside — it's whether air can actually move from that section's intake to its exhaust without crossing a wall, firewall, or solid partition. A roofline with a visible break or a separate ridge is a strong hint there's a separate space, but the reverse isn't reliable: additions are sometimes tied into the existing roof deck without the attics being opened to each other.

## Measurement notes
Go into the attic and check, don't assume from the roofline. Walk or look for a path between sections — if there's a fire-rated wall (common between an attached garage and the house), a full-height partition, or any solid barrier running from the top plate to the roof deck, that's a separate space. For each separate space found: measure its own floor square footage and run attic-ventilation-ratio's calculation independently — don't add the square footage together and size one shared system. The common mistake is treating a house's total attic square footage as one pool and hitting the ratio in aggregate, which can leave an isolated section under-ventilated even though the "whole house" number looks fine.
