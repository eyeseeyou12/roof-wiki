## Description
Glazed unit in a framed opening in the roof. The critical distinction is mount type: (1) deck mount — sits directly on the roof deck, low profile, uses a manufacturer flashing kit matched to the covering, not suitable for low slopes; (2) curb mount — sits on a wood curb built up above the roof plane, standard for flat and low-slope, and the usual choice for replacements because you can often reuse the existing curb; (3) self-flashing — flashing integrated into the frame, generally limited to shingle roofs at 3:12 or steeper.

## Replacement notes
On a curb-mount replacement you can often keep the curb and change only the unit, which is a much smaller job. Deck-mount replacements need the correct flashing kit for the covering, and that kit is a separate part number from the skylight.

## Measurement notes
Mount type. Rough opening dimensions. Roof covering — flashing kits are specific to shingle, high-profile tile, or metal, and they are not interchangeable. Roof pitch. Fixed vs. venting, and if venting, manual / electric / solar.

## Disambiguation
"Self-flashing" is sometimes used as another name for deck mount, which is a genuine source of confusion between what's actually two different things: a distinct mount type in its own right, and a loose synonym for deck mount.
